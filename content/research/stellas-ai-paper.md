## Research integrity note

This paper describes the project's rationale, current design direction, and an evaluation agenda. It does not report a controlled trial or claim measured improvements in grades, learning outcomes, or examination performance. Project usage figures are identified as internally reported and require validation before formal publication.

## Abstract

Secondary-school learners in Uganda study within a changing curriculum and assessment environment, while opportunities for individualised explanation and feedback can vary across classrooms, subjects, and schools. Generative artificial intelligence (AI) can provide on-demand assistance, but general-purpose systems may give inaccurate answers, overlook local curriculum requirements, misjudge student reasoning, or encourage answer copying.

This paper presents Stellas AI, a developing educational AI platform intended to support Ugandan O-Level and A-Level learners through curriculum-grounded explanations, practice questions, response feedback, and learning-progress support. The paper situates the project within UNESCO guidance on human-centred and age-appropriate AI, UNESCO's caution that education technology must be evaluated by learning outcomes, and Uganda's transition toward competency-based learning and assessment.

Uganda's 2025 Education Statistical Abstract reports a national secondary student–teacher ratio of 31:1 and a student–classroom ratio of 65:1, though these national indicators do not describe every school or classroom (Ministry of Education and Sports [MoES], 2025). Stellas is being developed around NCDC learning outcomes and curriculum materials, with a planned process for reviewing relevant NCDC and UNEB publications as assessment requirements evolve.

The paper describes the system's design principles, early product maturity, safeguards, limitations, and a future evaluation framework. Early project engagement—approximately 500 clicks over three months, as reported by the development team—is a usage signal only, not evidence of educational impact. The central proposition is that a locally grounded AI tutor may complement teachers by offering additional opportunities for explanation and practice, provided its curriculum alignment, answer quality, accessibility, privacy, and learning effects are independently evaluated.

## 1. Introduction

Generative AI has made conversational assistance available to learners outside formal lessons. A student can ask a question, request an explanation, test an idea, or receive feedback at a time that may not coincide with access to a teacher. The educational value of that access, however, depends on more than a fluent response. A learning assistant must explain accurately, support independent thinking, use appropriate examples, recognise misconceptions, and help learners practise the competencies that their curriculum expects.

Stellas AI began from a practical question: could an AI learning companion be designed specifically for Ugandan secondary-school students, rather than relying exclusively on general-purpose tools to interpret local curricula and examinations?

This paper records the rationale and current design direction of Stellas as a development-oriented case. It does not claim that AI can solve structural problems such as teacher shortages, school connectivity, or unequal access to learning materials. Instead, it explores a narrower contribution: a curriculum-grounded digital assistant that may extend opportunities for individual practice and feedback beyond the time available in a lesson.

## 2. Context: Uganda's secondary education environment

### 2.1 Curriculum and assessment are changing

Uganda introduced its lower-secondary competency-based curriculum from 2020. The NCDC describes the framework as organised around learning outcomes, values, generic skills, cross-cutting issues, and a curriculum menu, rather than content coverage alone (NCDC, n.d.).

### 2.2 Personalised support and classroom constraints

The Ministry of Education and Sports' Education Statistical Abstract 2025 reports a national secondary student–teacher ratio of 31:1. The student–classroom ratio is 65:1 nationally (MoES, 2025).

### 2.3 Local relevance and digital inclusion

UNESCO's 2023 Global Education Monitoring Report argues that technology can extend access to learning but can also exclude learners when meaningful connectivity is unequal.

## 3. Related guidance and research

UNESCO's Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research calls for a human-centred approach, ethical validation, age-appropriate use, and protection of personal data (Miao & Holmes, 2023).

## 4. Stellas AI: platform concept and design

### 4.1 Product purpose

Stellas AI is being developed as an AI learning companion for Uganda's O-Level and A-Level learners. The design aim is to shift AI use from answer production towards a study loop: ask, understand, practise, respond, receive feedback, and try again.

### 4.2 Curriculum-grounded content

Stellas uses NCDC learning outcomes and curriculum/textbook materials as part of its educational knowledge base.

### 4.3 Intended learning workflow

1. **Orient.** The student identifies a subject, topic, question, or learning goal.
2. **Explain.** Stellas offers a curriculum-relevant explanation.
3. **Practise.** The student attempts questions.
4. **Respond and receive feedback.** The system considers reasoning, not only final answers.
5. **Revisit.** Further hints or targeted practice address gaps.
6. **Reflect.** Progress indicators help decide what to revise next.

## 5. Theory of change

A curriculum-grounded tutor can increase access to relevant explanations and deliberate practice outside scheduled lessons. Each link in this chain is an assumption that requires evaluation.

## 6. Early development status

The development team reports approximately 500 clicks over three months. This is an engagement signal only.

## 7. Responsible AI and data governance

Requirements include accuracy and uncertainty handling, learning before answer completion, curriculum provenance, assessment fairness, privacy and access control, and student research consent.

## 8. Evaluation framework

A three-stage evaluation is recommended: curriculum validation, student usability, and learning-outcome pilot.

## 9. Discussion

The strongest research rationale is that a locally grounded learning assistant can make the relationship between curriculum outcomes, practice, feedback, and next steps more explicit for secondary learners.

## 10. Limitations

This is a development-oriented case description, not a peer-reviewed experimental study. No controlled student learning-outcome data are available.

## 11. Conclusion

Stellas AI is being developed around a locally important goal. Its value will ultimately depend on evidence, not intention.
